Why MUSD needs its own books

Mezo lets people borrow MUSD against their Bitcoin instead of selling it. That is useful, but it leaves an accounting gap: a borrower, a small business that gets paid in MUSD, and their accountant all need a clean, checkable record of what came in, what went out, what was borrowed and what was repaid. A block explorer shows raw logs. MUSD Books turns them into a statement that can be verified by someone who does not trust the sender.

What a statement contains

  • Exact amounts. Every Transfer of the token is counted once at full 18-decimal precision, using BigInt.
  • Reconciliation to the last unit. opening + in − out is compared with the onchain balance at the end of the period (read with balanceOf at the exact opening and closing blocks). For a plain ERC-20 like MUSD the difference is zero, and the statement says so. A non-zero value would expose a rebasing or fee-on-transfer token.
  • Mezo-aware labels. Tokens newly minted to the account are marked mint (for a Trove owner this is a loan draw; for protocol accounts it can be fee income) and tokens sent to the zero address are marked burn (for a Trove owner, a repayment). Core Mezo contracts such as BorrowerOperations, StabilityPool and PCV are named.
  • Invoice references. Mezo has no memo precompile. MusdPay moves tokens with transferFrom and emits PaymentReference as the log immediately after the token's own Transfer. The statement joins the two by log position, requiring payer, merchant and amount to match.
  • Deterministic CSV. The same address, token and block range always produce the same bytes, so the keccak256 hash identifies the statement.

Anchoring and verification

The hash can be anchored in StatementRegistry (several parties can attest the same hash). Verification is one view call plus an optional re-derivation from public chain data. Nothing leaves your browser except RPC calls.
